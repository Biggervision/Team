# MASTER VIDEO EDITING PROMPT — SK MAHBUB
Reusable Editing System | Consistent Personal Branding

## 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
Edit my uploaded video using the same visual identity, typography, motion style, color grading, sound design, and overall editing approach as my approved final edited video.

The approved final edited video is the master style reference. Reproduce its established editing style as closely as possible for every new video.

This is a reusable editing template. Adapt only the content-specific elements, including spoken topics, captions, highlighted keywords, animations, sound effects, and relevant visual elements, according to the instructions provided for each new video.

Do not redesign the entire editing style or introduce a completely different visual direction unless explicitly instructed.

## 2. ORIGINAL FOOTAGE AND VOICE
Preserve my original footage, face, natural appearance, voice, and intended message.

Do not replace my image with AI-generated footage or unnecessarily alter my facial features, skin tone, or appearance.

Maintain natural lip synchronization and clear voice audio. Improve the editing around my footage without making the video look artificial.

Use relevant visual enhancements alongside my original footage rather than replacing it with unnecessary graphics or backgrounds.

## 3. BRAND COLORS AND BACKGROUND DESIGN
Use my established brand color palette consistently.

- Primary brand color: #F6FBF9
- Upper gradient: #FFFFFF
- Lower gradient: #E6F3EE
- Primary text on light backgrounds: #172B26
- Accent color for checkmarks and selected highlights: #0D9488
- Optional blue accent for relevant numerical or data graphics: #2563EB

Apply a smooth vertical gradient to the designated blank canvas sections, transitioning from white at the top to my light brand color and a slightly deeper mint shade toward the bottom.

Add a subtle grid pattern with understated animation, gentle movement, or soft light sweeps where appropriate.

Important: Grid patterns, glassmorphism, translucent panels, and frosted-glass effects belong exclusively to the designated blank branded canvas scenes.

Do not apply glassmorphism to my original footage or place glass panels behind captions on my face-to-camera scenes.

## 4. TYPOGRAPHY AND CAPTION STYLE
Follow the caption style of my approved final edited video.

For scenes containing my original footage:

- Use clean white typography.
- Use a modern, professional sans-serif font.
- Combine regular-weight supporting text with slightly larger, bold keywords.
- Maintain clear text hierarchy, spacing, and readability.
- Position captions carefully so they do not cover my face.
- Keep the typography natural, minimal, and consistent with the approved reference.

Highlight the most important words and phrases instead of making every word equally prominent.

For blank branded canvas scenes, use deep forest green text and appropriate accent colors to maintain contrast against the light gradient background.

Do not introduce unnecessary text backgrounds, glass panels, decorative frames, or unrelated graphic elements.

## 5. TEXT ANIMATION AND MOTION GRAPHICS
Reproduce the refined animation style of the approved final video.

Use context-appropriate animations such as:

- Smooth fade-in and fade-out.
- Subtle upward movement.
- Clean word-by-word text reveals.
- Gentle scale-in effects for important keywords.
- Smooth transitions between text elements.
- Subtle zoom-in or punch-in effects for important spoken statements.

Choose animations based on the meaning, timing, and importance of the content.

Avoid aggressive bouncing, excessive scaling, distracting text movements, repetitive effects, and animations that feel artificial.

For footage scenes, keep the animation clean and typography-focused. Reserve more decorative animated grid and glass effects for blank canvas scenes.

## 6. PROFESSIONAL SOUND DESIGN
Sound design is an essential part of the editing process, not an optional enhancement.

Preserve my original voice as the primary audio element and build the sound design around the visual timeline.

Use relevant, well-timed sound effects for:

- Important caption and keyword reveals.
- Key statements and transitions.
- Number animations and statistics.
- Subtle zoom or emphasis moments.
- Motion graphics and branded canvas transitions.

Depending on the content, use appropriate whooshes, soft impacts, subtle clicks, risers, or other restrained sound effects.

Synchronize sound effects precisely with the corresponding visual action.

Use background music only when it complements the content. Keep it subtle and ensure that my voice remains clear and dominant.

Maintain smooth audio transitions, consistent volume, and a professional mix.

Avoid random sound effects, excessive impacts, distracting music, and unnecessary audio clutter.

## 7. CONTENT-DRIVEN EDITING
Analyze the actual spoken content before editing.

Identify the key message, important statements, statistics, questions, and moments that deserve additional visual emphasis.

Adapt the captions, highlighted keywords, sound effects, and animations to the subject of each new video.

Use numerical animations, charts, icons, or relevant motion graphics only when they genuinely help explain the message.

For example, a video about advertising budgets may use animated spending figures, while a video about conversion tracking may use relevant event-flow or analytics visuals.

Do not reuse content-specific graphics from previous videos when they are unrelated to the new topic.

The visual style must remain consistent, while the actual creative elements should change according to the content.

## 8. COLOR GRADING AND TRANSITIONS
Maintain the natural appearance of my original footage.

Use smooth, consistent color grading, realistic skin tones, balanced contrast, and a cohesive visual presentation.

Keep transitions between footage, captions, and branded canvas scenes fluid and intentional.

Use subtle zooms, motion, and transition effects only when they improve the storytelling.

Avoid harsh color changes, excessive saturation, unnatural skin tones, and unnecessarily dramatic effects.

## 9. VIDEO STRUCTURE AND ENDING
Maintain the original narrative and the natural flow of the spoken content.

Edit pacing according to the content, removing unnecessary dead space only when appropriate without cutting important words or changing the intended meaning.

Do not add unrelated agency branding, editing credits, watermarks, or promotional outros.

End the video naturally and cleanly, consistent with the approved editing style.

## 11. FINAL QUALITY CONTROL
Before delivering the video, verify that:

- The original footage and voice remain intact.
- The approved typography style is maintained.
- Glassmorphism and animated grids appear only on designated blank canvas scenes.
- Captions emphasize the correct keywords.
- Sound effects synchronize with visual animations.
- Audio remains balanced and speech is clearly audible.
- Brand colors and gradient backgrounds remain consistent.
- Transitions, animations, and color grading feel cohesive.
- Content-specific visuals are relevant to the new video.
- No unnecessary branding, credits, or unrelated elements have been added.

## FINAL DIRECTIVE
Treat my approved final edited video as the permanent style reference for this project.

For every subsequent video, preserve the same editing identity, caption treatment, brand palette, sound design quality, motion language, and visual consistency.

Change the content, not the established style. Only modify the specific elements I request or those that genuinely need to adapt to the new video's subject.

Deliver a complete, polished video that feels like another production from the same personal brand, with consistent quality and a natural, professional presentation.
