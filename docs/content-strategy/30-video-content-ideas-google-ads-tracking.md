### CATEGORY A — GOOGLE ADS + LOCAL SERVICE BUSINESSES

#### #1 — "Your Google Ads can look profitable while your business is losing money"
**Key talking points**
1. Google Ads measures what happens *on the platform*: clicks, calls, forms. It doesn't know which became paid jobs.
2. A roofer with a $60 CPL can lose money if only 1 in 20 leads books; a $180 CPL can be great if 1 in 3 books a $12k job (label: hypothetical math).
3. The metric that matters: cost per booked job, and revenue per ad dollar.
4. You need one bridge: lead source in the CRM + job outcome.
5. Until that bridge exists, "profitable" in Google Ads is an assumption.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (6–8 min):** Walk through the full chain: click → lead → estimate → job → revenue. Show where data drops off at each step.
- **Shorts/Reels/TikTok (45s):** Hook → two hypothetical CPLs on screen → "Which is better?" → reveal close rate flips it.
- **LinkedIn (90s):** Framed for owners and marketing managers: "What your agency report can't tell you."

**Why it matters:** The core thesis of the brand: platform metrics ≠ business results. Sets up every later post.

#### #2 — Calls from outside the service area
**Key talking points**
1. Story opener: a call from someone in a different state asking for service.
2. Location option "Presence or interest" can show ads to people researching your area from elsewhere.
3. Emergency trades (locksmiths, garage door, plumbers) feel this most: every wasted call is a dispatcher's time.
4. Check: Locations → "Where your users were" report, and location options under campaign settings.
5. Also check radius vs. ZIP targeting overlap and excluded areas.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (5 min):** Live screen walkthrough of where to find the setting and report.
- **Shorts (30–45s):** Phone ringing → "Hi, I'm in Ohio…" → the one setting.
- **LinkedIn (60s):** "A 2-minute check most local businesses never do."

**Why it matters:** Instant, verifiable win. Viewers check, find it, and credit you, which builds trust fast.

#### #3 — Paying for people who want to *become* a plumber
**Key talking points**
1. Keyword ≠ search term. Broad/phrase match can show you for related searches.
2. Common waste: jobs/salary/training, DIY/how-to, parts/supply, competitor names, free/cheap.
3. Search Terms report = what people actually typed.
4. Negative keywords block the wrong intent. Review regularly, not once.
5. The goal isn't fewer clicks; it's clicks from people with a problem you fix.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (6 min):** Read through a real (blurred) search terms report, categorize live.
- **Shorts (45s):** Rapid-fire "you paid for this search" with funny real terms on screen.
- **LinkedIn (60–90s):** "The report that shows what you're really paying for."

**Why it matters:** Highly shareable, a bit funny, and shows expertise without jargon.

#### #4 — 40 leads, 5 real opportunities
**Key talking points**
1. Start with the outcome, not the settings: why didn't 35 leads become opportunities?
2. Tag every bad lead: outside area, wrong service, price shopper, spam, no answer, duplicate.
3. Map tags to causes: intent (keywords), geography (location), expectation (ad/landing page), friction (form), signal (tracking), process (follow-up).
4. The biggest tag bucket tells you where to start.
5. "More leads" fixes nothing if the leak is downstream.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (8–10 min):** Full "lead autopsy" method with a template.
- **Shorts (60s):** "35 bad leads, 7 possible reasons." Quick list with icons.
- **LinkedIn (90–120s):** Diagnostic framework + downloadable tagging sheet.

**Why it matters:** Gives owners a method, not a tip. Positions you as a diagnostician.

#### #5 — CASE STUDY: Leads went up, revenue didn't
**Story structure**
- **Client situation:** Recurring/one-off service business (cleaning / junk removal / landscaping) running Search ads.
- **Business problem:** Monthly report showed lead growth; revenue was flat.
- **What the data showed:** Conversion actions counted any call (including very short ones) and all form submits, including spam.
- **What was actually wrong:** Google was optimizing toward the easiest "conversions", not booked jobs.
- **What changed:** Tightened conversion definitions (e.g., minimum call length, spam filtering, qualified-lead import from CRM).
- **Business outcome:** Use your own client data here. Do not state a number you can't show.
- **Lesson:** What you count is what you get.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (8–10 min):** Full story with blurred screenshots.
- **Shorts (60–90s):** Problem → twist ("the leads weren't really leads") → lesson.
- **LinkedIn (120s + text):** Story post with 3 lessons in the caption.

**Why it matters:** Proof of thinking, not just doing. Shows you diagnose before you change things.

#### #6 — Before you increase your Google Ads budget, check these 5 things
**Key talking points**
1. Search terms: are you paying for the right searches?
2. Locations: are leads coming from where you work?
3. Conversion accuracy: does a Google "conversion" equal a real lead?
4. Lead-to-job rate: do you know it by campaign?
5. Follow-up speed and answer rate: can the team handle more leads?

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (8 min):** Each check demonstrated in an account.
- **Shorts (60s):** Fast checklist, one per 10 seconds.
- **LinkedIn:** Carousel PDF + 90s video. Strong save potential.

**Why it matters:** Natural bridge to service: "If 2+ fail, diagnose first." Helpful, not salesy.

#### #7 — Google is doing exactly what you told it to do
**Key talking points**
1. Analogy: hiring a salesperson and paying them per business card collected, not per sale.
2. Smart Bidding learns patterns from the conversions you send.
3. Weak signal (any click/form) → Google finds more of the easiest actions.
4. Strong signal (qualified lead, booked job via offline import) → Google learns what a customer looks like.
5. Before blaming the algorithm, check the instruction.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (6–8 min):** Analogy → diagram → where to see bidding strategy and primary conversions.
- **Shorts (45s):** Business-card analogy alone carries it.
- **LinkedIn (90s):** For marketers: "Signal quality > bid strategy."

**Why it matters:** Reframes a universal frustration and links Google Ads to tracking, your unique overlap.

#### #8 — Your cheapest leads might be your most expensive
**Key talking points**

2. Show hypothetical math: CPL → close rate → cost per booked job → average job value.
3. Junk removal/moving/cleaning are highly price-compared, so lead quality varies a lot.
4. Sometimes paying more per lead is the profitable move.
5. Judge campaigns by cost per booked job.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (5–6 min):** Spreadsheet walkthrough.
- **Shorts (45s):** Two numbers on screen, reveal.
- **LinkedIn (60–90s):** "The metric your agency should report instead of CPL."

**Why it matters:** Counter-intuitive, easy to remember, changes how owners judge reports.

#### #9 — Google said Campaign A was best. The CRM said the opposite.
**Key talking points**
1. Platform ranks campaigns by conversions; business should rank by revenue.
2. Show campaign table with CRM columns added (label hypothetical if not real).
3. Typical flip: "repairs" campaign = volume; "replacement/full remodel" campaign = revenue.
4. How to build it: source/campaign captured on lead (e.g., UTM/GCLID) → stored in CRM → matched at month end.
5. Budget follows revenue, not volume.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (7–9 min):** Build the comparison table step by step.
- **Shorts (60s):** Table reveal: "Same account, two stories."
- **LinkedIn (90s + image):** Strong for marketing managers.

#### #10 — CASE STUDY: The ads were working at 11pm. Nobody was answering.
**Story structure**
- **Client situation:** Emergency service (water damage / plumbing / locksmith) running 24/7 ads.
- **Business problem:** Owner felt "Google leads are bad"; few jobs from paid calls.

- **What was actually wrong:** Ads ran when nobody answered. The problem was operational, not the targeting.
- **What changed:** Ad schedule aligned to answer coverage / after-hours answering service added; bid adjustments by hour.
- **Business outcome:** Use your own client data here.
- **Lesson:** Look at the whole path, not just the ad.

**Platform adaptation**

- **Shorts (60s):** Phone ringing at night visual → "Who answered?"
- **LinkedIn (90–120s):** "Marketing problems that aren't marketing problems."

**Why it matters:** Shows business-level thinking beyond ad settings. Builds trust with owners.

#### #11 — Your leads dropped. Don't touch the campaigns yet.
**Key talking points**
1. Step 1: Is tracking still working? (Test-submit, check conversion status.)
2. Step 2: Budget, billing, "limited by budget", disapproved ads.

#### #16 — Google says 50. CRM says 32.
**Key talking points**
1. Every system counts differently; some gap is expected.
2. Normal causes: spam, duplicates the CRM merges, attribution windows, phone leads not logged.
3. Problem signals: gap suddenly changes, gap is huge, platform > CRM consistently with no explanation.
4. Decide your "source of truth" for business decisions (usually CRM).
5. Reconcile monthly: same date range, same definition of lead.

#### #17 — One form, two leads
**Key talking points**
1. Start with the experience: you get one email notification, Google shows two.
2. Common causes: tag installed twice (site code + GTM), thank-you page reload, two primary conversion actions for the same lead, GA4 import + Google Ads tag both counted.
3. Impact: CPL looks better than reality → budget goes to the wrong places.
4. Then introduce the technical terms: counting setting ("One" vs. "Every" per click), primary vs. secondary actions, deduplication (e.g., transaction/event IDs).
5. Test: submit once, watch GTM Preview and conversions.

**Platform adaptation**

- **LinkedIn (90s):** For marketers auditing accounts.

#### #18 — Leads coming in, Google sees nothing
**Key talking points**
1. List contact paths: call from ad, call from website, form, chat, text, booking widget, email.
2. Often only some are tracked; calls from the website are commonly missed.
3. Missing signals → Smart Bidding thinks a campaign is failing → reduces delivery to what actually works.
4. Fixes: website call tracking (forwarding numbers), form tracking on the real success event, offline imports.
5. Audit: every path → is it tracked → where does it show?

**Platform adaptation**

#### #19 — Telling Google to find the wrong customers
**Key talking points**
1. The "Primary" conversion list is the instruction manual for Smart Bidding.
2. Common wrong instructions: click-to-call button clicks, contact page views, "scroll" or "engaged session" events.
3. Button click ≠ call completed ≠ qualified call.
4. Move micro-actions to secondary (observe, don't optimize).
5. Primary = actions closest to revenue.

**Platform adaptation**

#### #20 — Cookies explained without jargon
**Key talking points**
1. Analogy: a name tag the website hands your browser, which it shows next visit.
2. What it connects: ad click → visit → return visit → form.
3. What it doesn't know: it's the same *browser*, not necessarily the same *person*. Phone vs. laptop = different.
4. Limits: users delete them, browsers restrict them, consent choices affect them.
5. First-party vs. third-party, simply: your own website's note vs. someone else's.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (6–8 min):** Live demo in browser DevTools on your own site.
- **Shorts (45–60s):** Name-tag analogy with props.
- **LinkedIn (90s):** "What every business owner should understand about cookies."

**Why it matters:** Foundational education; unlocks understanding for #21, #22, #26, #27.

#### #21 — Cookie lifetime and why your ads may not get credit
**Key talking points**
1. Scenario: customer sees ad → visits → leaves → returns 3 weeks later directly → books.
2. Whether the ad gets credit depends on: was the identifying cookie still there? Did the browser shorten it? Same device? Consent given? Is the return inside the platform's conversion window?
3. No universal duration: cookie lifetime depends on the specific cookie, platform, browser and configuration. Some browsers limit how long certain cookies persist.
4. Long decision cycles (roofing, remodeling, flooring) are most exposed to under-crediting.
5. What to do: know your typical decision time, check conversion window settings, capture source at lead time and store it in your CRM (first-party).

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (8–10 min):** Animated timeline with branches.
- **Shorts (60–90s):** Story of one customer, two possible endings.
- **LinkedIn (120s):** Marketing-manager framing: "Why long-cycle businesses under-credit ads."

**Why it matters:** Rare topic explained clearly. Strong authority builder for sophisticated viewers.

#### #22 — Why Google misses conversions that really happened
**Key talking points**
1. Start: customer submitted the form, you got the email, Google didn't count it.
2. Browser tracking runs in the visitor's browser: ad blockers, privacy features, consent choices, slow loading or leaving the page early can stop it.
3. Server-side measurement: your server/system sends the conversion to the platform, so it's less dependent on the browser.
4. Not a magic fix: consent still applies; setup quality matters; can create duplicates if browser + server aren't deduplicated.
5. Who needs it: businesses where platform-reported conversions are consistently below real leads.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (8–10 min):** Diagram-heavy explainer.
- **Shorts (60s):** "The browser is the middleman, and it sometimes drops the message."
- **LinkedIn (90–120s):** For agency owners and marketers evaluating server-side.

**Why it matters:** Positions you in the advanced end of the market without scaring non-technical viewers.

#### #23 — Did your tracking survive the redesign?
**Key talking points**
1. Tracking often depends on specific URLs, button IDs, or form code.
2. Redesigns, new form plugins, Shopify/WordPress changes, new landing pages can break it.
3. No alarm goes off; numbers just drop, or quietly double.
4. Post-launch checklist: test every form and call path, check GTM Preview, check Google Ads conversion status, compare to CRM for 2 weeks.
5. Best practice: include tracking QA in the launch plan.

**Platform adaptation**
- **YouTube (6 min):** Post-launch QA checklist walkthrough.
- **Shorts (45s):** "New website. Same tracking? Maybe not."
- **LinkedIn (60–90s):** Share with web designers/agencies too.

**Why it matters:** Very common, timely trigger for help. Strong demand driver.

#### #24 — CASE STUDY: The "lead drop" that wasn't
**Story structure**
- **Client situation:** Service business (HVAC / plumbing / garage door) launched a refreshed website.
- **Business problem:** Google Ads conversions fell sharply within days; campaigns started spending less efficiently.
- **What the data showed:** CRM leads and call volume were stable or up. Conversion drop aligned exactly with the launch date.
- **What was actually wrong:** New form showed an inline success message instead of a thank-you page; the tag was triggered by the old page URL.
- **What changed:** Trigger rebuilt on the actual form success event; tested in GTM Preview; verified in Google Ads.
- **Business outcome:** Use your own client data here.
- **Lesson:** When results change the same week the website does, check tracking first.

**Platform adaptation**

- **LinkedIn (120s + text):** Story post.

**Why it matters:** Proves tracking is a business issue, not a technical detail.

#### #25 — CASE STUDY: When worse numbers are better
**Story structure**
- **Client situation:** Service business with "great" Google Ads CPL.
- **Business problem:** Strong reported CPL, but leads in the CRM didn't match the volume.
- **What the data showed:** Conversions roughly double CRM leads; one submission triggered multiple counted conversions.
- **What was actually wrong:** Duplicate tags + two primary conversion actions for the same lead.
- **What changed:** Single source of truth, correct counting, secondary for duplicates.
- **Business outcome:** Reported CPL rose to its true level; decisions now based on real data. Use your own client data here.
