SECTION 01

Landing Page Analysis

The page sells one thing: a non-invasive, medication-free sleep treatment programme, entered through a free initial consultation. Everything in this campaign is built backwards from that sentence.

1.1 The treatment programme

AVWF® Neurostimulation, presented on the page as "Schlafstörungen – Behandlungsprogramm". Specially modulated sound signals are delivered through precisely calibrated therapy headphones. The page is explicit that these are "keine Entspannungsmusik, sondern gezielte Impulse". The signals act via the fine muscles of the inner ear and the cranial nerves originating there, reaching the regulatory circuits that govern the switch between wakefulness and rest.

The stated sequence: "zuerst die Überaktivierung senken, dann den beruhigenden Vagustonus aufbauen, zuletzt die Belastbarkeit festigen." The page positions the therapy as working on the nervous system itself rather than on symptoms — "nicht mit Schlafmitteln oder gegen die Symptome, sondern direkt am Nervensystem selbst" — and describes it as nicht invasiv und medikamentenfrei.

1.3 Target audience

Adults in Zurich with chronic, self-identified sleep problems who have already tried the obvious routes. The page tells you this directly: it positions AVWF as the foundation that makes everything else work — "Erst wenn das Nervensystem zur Ruhe findet, können Schlafhygiene, ärztliche Behandlung oder Entspannungsverfahren ihre volle Wirkung entfalten." That sentence is written for someone who has already tried sleep hygiene, or medication, or both, and is still awake at 3 a.m.

Secondary overlap, confirmed on the practice page: stress, Erschöpfung, Burnout, ADHS. The practice treats children as well as adults, but this page is written for adults.

1.4 Benefits the page claims (and only these)

Listed on the page under "Die Wirkung": schnelleres Einschlafen · das Gedankenkarussell kommt zur Ruhe · ruhigeres Durchschlafen mit weniger nächtlichem Erwachen · tieferer, erholsamer Schlaf und echte nächtliche Regeneration · erholtes Aufwachen und mehr Energie über den Tag · der Teufelskreis aus Anspannung und Schlaflosigkeit wird durchbrochen.

Critical finding — AVWF cannot be booked online

On /online-buchen, four therapies (Augenuntersuchung, Longevity Medizin, Physiotherapie, TCM) each have a working "Online buchen" button. AVWF Neurostimulation has only a phone number and an "E-Mail schreiben" link. This has two direct consequences for the campaign:

  • The conversion goal cannot be "booking completed". It must be contact intent — call, form, email.
  • The header "BUCHEN" button is a dead end for exactly the visitor this campaign sends. Fix before launch (Section 10).

Scope correction

This programme is not physiotherapy. Physiotherapie is a separate service at the same practice, delivered by a different practitioner (Danijela Campbell). Writing physiotherapy-led ads against this page would break keyword-to-page relevance and waste budget. The campaign in this document targets the neurostimulation sleep programme only.

SECTION 02

Topic Selection

Two topics were selected from the page's own positioning. Both were chosen for lead quality, not search volume.

2.1 The two selected topics

Topic 1 — Sleep disorder treatment in Zurich

The searcher: someone who has already named their problem as a condition and is now looking for a provider who treats it, near them.

Query shape:schlafstörungen behandlung zürich · schlaftherapie zürich · hilfe bei schlafstörungen · schlafstörungen spezialist zürich

Why it is the strongest topic: the transition from "I sleep badly" to "Schlafstörungen behandeln" is the moment someone accepts they need professional help. That is the highest commercial intent available in this market. It also matches the page perfectly at every level — the H1 is literally Schlafstörungen, the section heading is Schlafstörungen – Behandlungsprogramm, and the title tag already reads Schlafstörungen in Zürich Stauffacher.

Topic 2 — Sleep therapy without medication

The searcher: someone who has already rejected or exhausted sleeping pills and is actively hunting for a drug-free alternative.

Query shape:schlafstörungen ohne medikamente · alternative zu schlafmitteln · medikamentenfreie schlaftherapie · neurostimulation schlaf

Why it is the strongest differentiator: this is the page's central promise, stated twice in its own words. These searchers arrive pre-qualified against the main objection — they have already decided they do not want pills, which is precisely what this programme is not. Volume is lower than Topic 1, but intent-to-enquire is the highest in the account, and there is far less competition from pharmacies and general practices on these terms.

2.2 What was considered and deliberately rejected

Why two ad groups and not five

Search volume for sleep-therapy terms inside the City of Zurich is modest. Splitting it across five ad groups starves each one of impressions, delays learning, and makes Quality Score data unreadable for months. Two tightly themed ad groups concentrate the data, keep ad-to-keyword relevance very high, and remain manageable for a non-technical operator. This is a deliberate choice, not a simplification.

SECTION 04

Targeting Considerations

4.1 Geographic targeting

DecisionRecommendation
TargetZürich, Zurich, Switzerland — the entry labelled City. Not Canton, not "Switzerland".
Location optionPresence (target), Presence (exclude). Change this manually — Google does not always default to it.
Radius at launchNo. At CHF 40/day you cannot test two variables at once. Prove city traffic converts first.
Exclusions at launchNone. The City target simply does not include the surrounding municipalities, so excluding them is redundant.
Phase 2 (week 4+)If city traffic converts, add a 10 km radius around Kanzleistrasse 4, 8004 Zürich as a second location. That covers Schlieren, Opfikon, Zollikon, Kilchberg, Adliswil — roughly 20 minutes by car or S-Bahn, the honest catchment for a programme requiring 10 in-practice hours. Do not exceed 15 km; beyond that, travel friction kills attendance.

Exactly where to set this

Campaigns → select the campaign → Settings → expand Locations → Enter another location → type Zürich → select the result labelled Zürich, Zurich, Switzerland — City → Target. Delete any pre-filled "Switzerland" entry. Then click Location options directly beneath the box and set Target to Presence: People in or regularly in your included locations.

To verify after launch: Insights and reports → When and where ads showed → Locations. Check this on day 7. Clicks from outside Zurich mean the Presence setting reverted.

4.2 Language and market considerations

  • Write in standard German. Swiss German is spoken, not typed. Zurich users search in standard German.
  • Swiss orthography: use ss, never ß. All copy in this document complies.
  • Umlaut variants (zuerich, schlafstoerungen) are handled automatically by Google's close-variant matching. No separate keywords needed.
  • Tone: the landing page is calm, precise and clinical — not emotive or salesy. The ad copy matches that register deliberately. A hype-heavy ad against this page would convert worse, not better.

4.3 Device considerations

Expect 55–70 % mobile traffic for a health search in this market. Mobile users will predominantly tap the call asset rather than fill in the footer form. This is the reason the call asset is treated as a primary conversion in Section 8, and the reason the above-the-fold CTA fix in Section 10 is classified as blocking.

4.4 Policy considerations

  • No phone numbers in ad text. Google editorial policy prohibits them in headlines, descriptions and sitelink descriptions. All copy in this document complies — use the call asset.
  • Healthcare vertical: ads may occasionally be caught by the automated healthcare filter. If an ad shows Disapproved, read the stated policy and appeal. No special certification is required for this type of practice.
  • No health-based audience targeting (see Section 3.2).
  • No unsupported claims anywhere: no cure, no guarantee, no superlative, no insurance claim, no price.

Pinning — Ad 1A

Pin headlines 1, 2 and 3 to Position 1. Nothing else. Pinning a pool of three guarantees the searcher's own term appears first — the strongest single driver of CTR and Ad Relevance — while still letting Google test within the pool. Ad Strength is only penalised heavily when a single asset is pinned per position. Do not pin descriptions; all four work in either slot.

5.3 Responsive Search Ad 1B — control ad

Create a second RSA in this ad group using the same 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, with no pinning at all. After 30 days, compare CTR and conversion rate between 1A and 1B. This isolates the effect of pinning in your account and tells you whether to pin across the rest of the account. Keep everything else identical — one variable at a time.

The single most important setting in this document

Set the call-conversion threshold to 60 seconds. A 15-second call is a wrong number or a hang-up. Sixty seconds means a real conversation started. Because booking an AVWF appointment is a phone conversation, this one setting is what separates a campaign optimised for qualified leads from one optimised for noise.

8.3 Avoiding duplicate and inflated conversions

  1. Set "Count: One" on every lead action. The default is "Every", which counts three conversions if someone taps the phone number three times. This is the single most common cause of inflated healthcare conversion data.
  2. One source per action. Either the native Google Ads tag or an imported GA4 key event — never both for the same thing. Recommended: native Google Ads tags via GTM for primary actions, GA4 for analysis only.
  3. Turn off Wix's built-in Google Ads conversion tracking if anyone has enabled it under Marketing Integrations. Two systems firing on the same form doubles every number.
  4. Accept one small overlap. Someone who clicks tel: on the site and calls from the ad can fire both actions 2 and 3. "Count: One" plus a 1-day window on action 2 keeps this to a rounding error. Do not over-engineer it.
  5. Untick account-default goals at campaign level so no legacy conversion action bleeds into bidding.

8.5 Closing the loop on lead quality

Google can tell you how many people contacted the practice. It cannot tell you how many became patients — and for a high-consideration therapy programme, that difference is the whole game.

Once a month, ask the practice two questions: how many enquiries arrived, and how many became paying patients. Divide ad spend by the second number. That figure — not the CPA in the Google Ads interface — is your true cost per patient, and it is the number that should drive every budget decision from month two onward.

Offline conversion import via gclid would automate this, but it requires a CRM the practice does not appear to have. Do not attempt it in month one.
